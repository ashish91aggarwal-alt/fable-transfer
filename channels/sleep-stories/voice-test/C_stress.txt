Her name was Mrs Penrose, and she had kept the inn at Saltmere Head for longer than she could easily count.

The path wound past gorse and heather, over a wooden stile, and down towards the oilskin-coloured sea.

"Well now," she said. "You've walked a long way, by the look of you. Come in, come in, before the weather follows you."

"It's a good few steps," said Tom. "I used to count them. These days I just let my feet remember the way."

Wind, south-westerly, light. Rain, steady. Sea, calm.

A pair of old boots stood by the step, filled with geraniums that had kept on flowering into the autumn, red and stubborn.

In between, there was the great glass lens to polish, and the wicks to trim. And all through the long nights, winter and summer, there was the clockwork to wind, to keep the light turning.

Patter, patter, on the roof... Breathing in... And out...

And round. And round. And round.
