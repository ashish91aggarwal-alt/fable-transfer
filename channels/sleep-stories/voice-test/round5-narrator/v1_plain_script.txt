The coast path ran along the top of the cliffs like a pale ribbon someone had dropped in the grass. On one side, the land rolled away in fields of heather and gorse. On the other side, far below, lay the sea, grey as a pigeon's wing, breathing in and out against the rocks.

Mara had been walking since the middle of the afternoon. She was a mapmaker, and she had spent the autumn walking this stretch of coast with a notebook in her coat pocket, drawing every cove and stile and crooked signpost. She liked the work. She liked the slowness of it. You couldn't hurry a map. You had to walk every step of it yourself.
