# Music for the sleep-story channel: research and plan (v1, 15 Nov 2026)

**Bottom line:** music should be a quiet bed under the voice, not a feature. Use slow, beatless or 60–80 bpm instrumental music with simple, smooth melodies, mixed at least 20 dB below the narration. It fades into rain and sea as the listener falls asleep. Build a recognisable channel sound from melodies that feel familiar to UK, US and Australian listeners (folk lullaby airs, soft piano, harp). Indian raga ideas go in as a subtle drone and phrasing colour, not as the main style. Skip binaural beats and "healing frequency" claims.

## 1. What the science says

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Music that helps sleep is slow (60–80 bpm), soft and smooth, instrumental and simple in structure, often classical or new age | Pan & Wang 2025, narrative review of RCTs (Frontiers in Sleep) — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sleep/articles/10.3389/frsle.2025.1707162/full |
| Listening reduced time to fall asleep and increased total sleep; best dose about 30–45 min before bed at comfortable volume | same review |
| Cochrane 2022 (13 RCTs, 1,007 adults with insomnia): moderate-certainty improvement in sleep quality | cited in the same review |
| Cultural familiarity and nature sounds also helped in specific contexts | same review |
| Calm music beat rhythmic music for sleep in older adults | Chen et al. 2021, J Am Geriatr Soc — https://www.healthinaging.org/blog/does-listening-to-calming-music-at-bedtime-actually-help-you-sleep/ |
| Binaural beats: brain-entrainment evidence inconsistent (8 of 14 EEG studies found none); need headphones | Ingendoh et al. 2023, PLOS ONE; Capezuti et al. 2022 found no strong evidence for auditory stimulation in general |

What this means for us: the 30–45 min listening dose matches our 45–60 min stories. No beat, no surprises, no lyrics under the narration.

## 2. India: what its music history teaches

- **Time theory of ragas (samay):** Hindustani music assigns ragas to times of day. Night ragas include Yaman (6–9 pm), Bageshree (9 pm–midnight), and Malkauns and Darbari Kanada (after midnight), described as tranquil, deep, inward. Sources: https://www.hindustaniclassical.com/article/the-time-theory-of-ragas:-a-confluence-of-sound,-science,-and-sentiment, https://artiumacademy.com/music-theory/hindustani-classical/ragas
- **The lullaby raga:** in Carnatic music, Neelambari is the traditional lullaby raga, with flowing, blended ornaments. Kerala's famous lullaby "Omanathinkal Kidavo" (c. 1813) is often sung in it.
- **Evidence check:** a 1998 sleep-lab study found Neelambari did not change sleep compared with another raga (Indian J Physiol Pharmacol 42(1):119–22). Its sleepy effect is probably learned from hearing lullabies sung in it.
- **Lesson:** sleepiness comes from slowness, familiarity and association, not from a magic scale. For a UK/US/AU audience, a raga is unfamiliar, so it would not carry that association.
- **What we borrow:** the tanpura-style drone (one steady, warm tone that never changes key) and slow, gliding alap-like phrases with no rhythm. Both are calming without needing cultural knowledge, and they give the channel a sound nobody else in the niche has. Raga scales and named ragas are kept for a possible India-focused channel later.

## 3. UK

- **Folk airs and the harp:** the Welsh melody "Ar Hyd y Nos" ("All Through the Night") was first printed in 1784 and became a lullaby; it is a harp tradition. Source: https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ar_Hyd_y_Nos. Old melodies like this are public domain; a new performance we make is ours.
- **Brahms' Lullaby** (1868) is the world's best-known cradle song. Public-domain composition, but every existing recording is copyrighted.
- **Ambient was invented in Britain:** Brian Eno's *Music for Airports* (1978) defined ambient music as able to "accommodate many levels of listening attention… as ignorable as it is interesting". That is exactly the job of music under a sleep story.
- **Palette:** harp, soft felt piano, cello or warm strings, gentle wind (low whistle), rain on glass.

## 4. US

- **Lullaby tradition:** "Hush, Little Baby" (Southern US) and "Rock-a-bye Baby" (printed 1765). Western lullabies are typically in 6/8 or triple time, which mimics rocking.
- **Americana palette:** fingerpicked acoustic guitar, mountain dulcimer, soft piano, distant harmonica pad (very low), crickets and night ambience.
- **Market reference:** Calm's Sleep Stories mix "music, sound effects, and incredible voice acting", typically 20–40 min, with most stories "paired with subtle, calming background music". Sources: https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005140554-Sleep-Stories, https://www.sleepfoundation.org/best-sleep-apps/best-bedtime-stories-apps

## 5. Australia

- **Shares the UK/US palette** in practice (English-language folk, piano, ambient).
- **Do not use the didgeridoo (yidaki) or Aboriginal songs.** Many Northern Aboriginal people object to its use by non-Indigenous people, and it is generally expected that non-Indigenous people seek permission and guidance before playing it. Using it as an "Australia" signal would be disrespectful and a reputational risk.
- **Australian feel instead comes from nature sound:** surf, bush rain, distant birds at dusk, which carry no cultural ownership issue.

## 6. How music upgrades the experience (the design)

| Moment | Music | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Short channel motif: 4–6 notes on harp or piano over the drone, the same in every episode | Brand recognition; regular listeners relax on hearing it (learned association, like the Neelambari effect) |
| Opening breathing | Drone plus a few slow notes, no melody to follow | Supports slow breathing |
| Story | Very soft bed, beatless or about 60 bpm, ducked to 20–24 dB below the voice; rises 3–4 dB only in long pauses | Voice stays clear on any speaker; music fills silences gently |
| Key cosy moments (fire, tea, rain) | Same bed, a touch warmer (one extra instrument) | Emotion without drama |
| Final fade | Music thins to the drone, then hands over to rain/sea | Matches the narrator's slowing |
| After the story (10–20 min) | Rain/sea only, or drone plus rain, dimming screen | Sleep without a jolt at the end; labelled honestly |

Rules: no beat or drums; no lyrics; no sudden notes, key changes or crescendos; one key per episode; no recognisable copyrighted melody; avoid 432 Hz and binaural marketing claims.

## 7. Rights and platform safety

- Make the music ourselves (written as code and rendered locally, or with an open-weight model) or perform public-domain melodies ourselves. Keep the project files as proof.
- AI music is generally not in Content ID today, but that may change; tools that label music "royalty-free" can still trigger claims. Sources: https://www.foximusic.com/blog/content-id-music-guide-monetization/, https://hellothematic.com/ai-music-for-youtube/
- Background-music channels face "No AI" backlash against generic AI tracks. A consistent, recognisable channel sound (motif, drone, instrument palette) protects against looking like mass-produced content.
